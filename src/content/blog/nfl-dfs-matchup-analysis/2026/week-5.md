## FAQ

### What makes a matchup "good" for DFS, beyond points allowed?

Points allowed to a position tells you how a defense performed against
that position historically — it doesn't tell you why, or whether it's
still true this week. A real matchup read combines the game's total,
the spread, and position-specific matchup multipliers, which account
for opponent quality directly rather than a season-long reputation. All
three move independently; using only one is how a real edge gets
missed.

### Why do close-spread games matter more than high totals alone?

A high total with a lopsided spread often means one offense dominates
the game script while the other gets abandoned early — real production
concentrated on one side, not spread across both. A close spread means
neither team is expected to pull away, so both offenses stay live
longer into the game.

### Where do the matchup multiplier numbers actually come from?

Each one is a defense's average DK fantasy points allowed to a
position, divided by the league-wide average allowed to that position
— a real, computed ratio, not a subjective read. Above 1.0 means that
defense has historically allowed more than average to that position; a
value under 1.0 means less. As of Week 5, this blends four real weeks
of current-season results with prior-season history — the deepest
current-season sample of the year so far. The
[matchup analysis hub](/blog/nfl-dfs-matchup-analysis/) covers this and
its other real limitations (small samples, defensive injuries, game
script blindness) in full.

### How often does this get updated before kickoff?

Whenever a line move or a new injury designation is significant enough
to matter. Check the "as of" line at the top of the post — it always
reflects the most recent real update, not just when this was first
published.
