## FAQ

### What makes a matchup good for DFS, beyond points allowed?

Points allowed to a position tells you how a defense performed against that position historically, not why, or whether it's still true this week. A matchup read combines the game's total, the spread, and position-specific matchup multipliers, which account for opponent quality directly rather than a season-long reputation. All three move independently; using only one is how an edge gets missed.

### Why do close-spread games matter more than high totals alone?

A high total with a lopsided spread often means one offense dominates the game script while the other gets abandoned early, production concentrated on one side instead of spread across both. A close spread means neither team is expected to pull away, so both offenses stay live longer into the game.

### Where do the matchup multiplier numbers actually come from?

Each one is a defense's average DK fantasy points allowed to a position, divided by the league-wide average allowed to that position, a computed ratio, not a subjective read. Above 1.0 means that defense has historically allowed more than average to that position; a value under 1.0 means less. As of Week 6, this blends five weeks of current-season results with prior-season history. The [matchup analysis hub](/blog/nfl-dfs-matchup-analysis/) covers this and its other limitations (small samples, defensive injuries, game script blindness) in full.

### How do I read a spread number like "BUF -5.5"?

The number after the team name is how many points that team is favored by. This post names the actual favorite directly for each game rather than printing a bare home/away-relative number.

### How often does this get updated before kickoff?

Whenever a line move or a new injury designation is significant enough to matter. A note at the top of the post marks any update after publication; no note means the numbers above are current as of publication.
