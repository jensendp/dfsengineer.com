A quarterback throws for 280 yards, two touchdowns, and one interception. Depending on the platform, that performance is worth anywhere from 18.2 to 22.2 fantasy points. The gap matters more than most beginners realize.

NFL DFS scoring rules are the foundation of every lineup decision you make. Get them wrong — or assume they're the same everywhere — and your player evaluations start from a flawed premise.

This breakdown covers the exact scoring systems on DraftKings, FanDuel, and PrizePicks for the 2026 NFL season, with worked examples that show how the same stat line produces different point totals and different optimal roster builds.

## How NFL DFS Scoring Differs from Season-Long Fantasy

If you've played season-long fantasy on ESPN or Yahoo, you already know the basics — touchdowns are good, turnovers are bad. But the specific point values don't carry over cleanly to DFS. The scoring systems on DraftKings, FanDuel, and PrizePicks are each proprietary, and none of them match the default settings on any major season-long platform. If you're still getting oriented on what DFS actually is and how contests are structured, the [complete beginner's breakdown](https://dfsengineer.com/blog/what-is-nfl-dfs-a-complete-beginners-breakdown-2026) covers that ground before diving into scoring specifics.

The single biggest structural difference: reception scoring. DraftKings awards 1 full point per reception (full PPR). FanDuel awards 0.5 points per reception (half-PPR). That gap sounds small until you run the numbers. A running back who catches 6 passes in a game earns 6 points from receptions alone on DraftKings but only 3 on FanDuel. Over a full slate of players, that distinction warps which positions carry the most value and which player profiles you should prioritize.

> **Tip:** If terms like "PPR," "half-PPR," "slate," or "ceiling" are unfamiliar, the [NFL DFS glossary](https://dfsengineer.com/blog/nfl-dfs-glossary) and [terminology guide with real examples](https://dfsengineer.com/blog/nfl-dfs-terminology-40-terms-with-real-examples) define all of them in context.

DFS does simplify one thing, though. In season-long leagues, your commissioner might use custom settings — 6-point passing touchdowns, fractional scoring turned off, odd bonus rules. In DFS, every contest on a given platform uses the same scoring. No guessing. You learn DraftKings scoring once and it applies to every DraftKings contest you enter in 2026.

That consistency is why understanding scoring platform by platform matters so much. It's the fixed foundation underneath every projection, every lineup build, every roster decision.

## DraftKings NFL Scoring: Full Breakdown

DraftKings scores passing yards at 0.04 points per yard (1 point per 25 yards), passing touchdowns at 6 points, and interceptions at −1. Rushing and receiving yards both earn 0.1 points per yard (1 point per 10 yards), with touchdowns worth 6 points in either category. Every reception scores 1 full point under DraftKings' full PPR system. Fumbles lost cost −1.

DST scoring uses a tiered points-allowed structure — 0 points allowed gets you 10 points, 1–6 allowed gets 7, and it scales down from there. Sacks score 1 point each, interceptions and fumble recoveries 2 each, and defensive touchdowns 6.

The bonus thresholds are where DraftKings scoring turns nonlinear. Hit 300 passing yards, 100 rushing yards, or 100 receiving yards and you pocket an extra 3 points. These bonuses make the difference between a solid night and a ceiling-busting one.

Here's a worked example to put this in concrete terms. A running back posts 85 rushing yards, 1 rushing TD, 4 receptions, and 38 receiving yards. That's 8.5 (rushing yards) + 6 (TD) + 4 (receptions) + 3.8 (receiving yards) = **22.3 DraftKings points**. No bonuses triggered — he fell 15 yards short of the 100-yard rushing threshold. Push that rushing line to 100 and the total jumps to 26.8: the extra 15 yards add 1.5 points directly, plus the 3-point bonus kicks in. That 15-yard difference is worth 4.5 points, not 1.5.

## FanDuel NFL Scoring: Full Breakdown

FanDuel's scoring system runs leaner than DraftKings in a few key spots. The core difference starts at receptions: 0.5 points per catch instead of 1.0. That single rule change ripples through every roster decision.

Here's the full FanDuel point structure for the 2026 NFL season:

**Passing:** 0.04 points per yard (1 point per 25 yards), 4 points per TD, −1 per interception.

**Rushing:** 0.1 points per yard, 6 points per TD, −2 per fumble lost.

**Receiving:** 0.1 points per yard, 6 points per TD, 0.5 points per reception.

**DST:** 6 points per defensive TD, 2 per sack, 2 per interception/fumble recovery, with points-allowed tiers ranging from +10 (zero points allowed) down to −4 (35+ allowed).

> **Warning:** FanDuel charges −2 per fumble lost, not −1 like DraftKings. That's a subtle difference that changes how you should think about fumble-prone players on FanDuel specifically.

No yardage bonuses exist on FanDuel. None. A quarterback throwing for 299 yards and one throwing for 301 yards earn proportional credit — there's no cliff at 300 where bonus points suddenly appear. This flattens the scoring curve and reduces the ceiling spikes that DraftKings rewards.

Now the worked example. Take that same running back: 85 rushing yards, 1 TD, 4 receptions, 38 receiving yards.

- Rushing: 85 × 0.1 = **8.5**
- Rushing TD: **6.0**
- Receptions: 4 × 0.5 = **2.0**
- Receiving yards: 38 × 0.1 = **3.8**
- **FanDuel total: 20.3 points**

On DraftKings, that identical stat line scores 22.3 — the extra 2.0 points come entirely from the reception difference (4 catches × 0.5 = 2.0 additional points on DraftKings). A back catching 5–6 passes per game gains more separation on DraftKings than FanDuel. The volume threshold where pass-catching backs become premium plays shifts between platforms, and ignoring that shift means mispricing players before you even set a lineup.

## PrizePicks and Other Platforms: Scoring Differences That Matter

PrizePicks calculates fantasy scores using a system that closely mirrors DraftKings full PPR — 1 point per reception, 0.04 points per passing yard, 6 points per passing touchdown, 6 points per rushing/receiving touchdown. But "closely mirrors" isn't "identical." PrizePicks does not award yardage bonuses (no 300-yard passing or 100-yard rushing/receiving bonuses), which compresses the ceiling for big games. That running back from our earlier example — 85 rushing yards, 1 TD, 4 receptions, 38 receiving yards — scores 22.3 on both PrizePicks and DraftKings for that stat line (no bonuses triggered either way). But push that rushing line to 105 yards and the totals diverge: DraftKings adds 2.0 yards points plus the 3-point bonus for 27.3, while PrizePicks adds only the 2.0 yards points for 24.3.

Underdog Fantasy also runs full PPR with no bonuses, making its scoring nearly interchangeable with PrizePicks for most calculations. SuperDraft uses a multiplier-based salary system, but its underlying point values track closer to half-PPR — putting it nearer to FanDuel's math.

This matters even in pick'em formats. When you're deciding whether a player goes over or under a posted line, knowing which scoring system generated that line is the difference between a sharp pick and a lazy one. If you're importing projections built on DraftKings scoring into a PrizePicks entry, you risk inflating ceilings for players near bonus thresholds.

Build separately for each scoring system. Stop copying lineups across platforms.

## Common NFL DFS Scoring Mistakes Beginners Make

The most expensive mistake is also the most common: pulling projections from one source and plugging them into a different platform without adjusting. A projection built on DraftKings full PPR scoring will overvalue a slot receiver by 3–5 points on FanDuel if that player catches 6–10 balls. That's not a rounding error — it's enough to wreck your entire roster construction. Before you use any projection set, confirm which scoring system it's built on. If you don't know, the projections are noise.

> **Tip:** If you use a projection source that doesn't label which scoring system it's built on, you can reverse-engineer it. Take a player with a known stat-line projection and manually calculate his score under DraftKings rules, then FanDuel rules. Whichever total matches the source's projected score tells you which system they used.

Fumble scoring trips people up, too. DraftKings charges −1 for a fumble lost, not just any fumble. A running back who puts the ball on the ground but recovers it himself takes no scoring hit. Interceptions cost quarterbacks −1 on both DraftKings and FanDuel — but beyond the direct penalty, the bigger cost is the touchdown that doesn't happen because the drive ended on a turnover.

DST scoring is where beginners lose the most edge without realizing it. Points-allowed tiers are not linear. On DraftKings, a defense allowing 1–6 points scores 7 fantasy points, but allowing 7–13 drops that to 4. A single garbage-time touchdown can swing your DST score by 3 points — something most beginners never price in. Once you understand how those tiers actually work, you stop treating all "good defensive matchups" as equal.

Both DraftKings and FanDuel use the same roster structure for NFL classic contests: 9 slots consisting of QB, RB, RB, WR, WR, WR, TE, FLEX, and a defense (labeled DST on DraftKings, DEF on FanDuel). Neither platform includes a kicker. The platforms differ in salary cap ($50,000 on DraftKings, $60,000 on FanDuel) and scoring rates — not in roster composition. Knowing this prevents a common beginner error: assuming FanDuel gives you an extra slot or different positional requirements. ## FAQ

### Is NFL DFS scoring the same as PPR fantasy football?

Not exactly. DraftKings uses full PPR — 1 point per reception — which matches some season-long leagues, but the similarities mostly end there. FanDuel uses half-PPR at 0.5 points per reception, and passing touchdowns are scored differently (6 points on DraftKings, 4 on FanDuel). DraftKings also awards a 3-point bonus when a passer hits 300 yards or a rusher/receiver hits 100 yards — something most ESPN or Yahoo leagues don't include by default. DST scoring uses platform-specific tiered structures for points allowed rather than a single linear scale. When someone says "it's PPR scoring," that tells you roughly one-third of what you need to know about how a given platform actually scores.

### How is the fantasy score calculated on PrizePicks?

PrizePicks uses a scoring system that closely mirrors DraftKings full PPR — 1 point per reception, 0.04 points per passing yard, 6 points per passing touchdown, 6 points per rushing/receiving touchdown. The key difference: no yardage bonuses. A quarterback throwing for 350 yards gets no extra credit beyond the per-yard points, unlike on DraftKings where he'd earn a 3-point bonus for crossing 300. This means PrizePicks over/under lines for high-volume players may sit slightly lower than you'd expect if you're used to DraftKings scoring.
