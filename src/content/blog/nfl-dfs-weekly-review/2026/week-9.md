*Results as of the Week 9 games. This grades the
[pre-game snapshot](/blog/nfl-dfs-weekly-signals/2026/week-9/) as
published. No projection changed after the fact.*

The pre-game post showed what the data looked like. This one grades it:
projections against actual DraftKings points, the hits and misses, and
whether the model earned its place on the board. One week is a small
sample, so this page shows the method and the numbers and declares no
winner.

## The short version

- On the 44-player board, the model's projections lined up worse with what actually happened than the public average did: correlation 0.51 vs 0.60, average miss 7.3 vs 6.3 points. The model was closer than the public average on 16 of 44 players.
- Across all 259 graded players (not just the board), the model beat the public sources on both measures: the model's average miss was 4.46 points vs 5.00 for the 4 public sources (their individual scores averaged), with correlation 0.659 vs 0.631.
- The board is a selected group, the top-scoring names, so its correlations run lower than a full slate's. Both views are shown for that reason.
- The largest model miss on the board was Jahmyr Gibbs (model 32.3, actual 13.4). The largest beat was Matthew Golden (model 14.4, actual 35.4).

## Board results by group

| Group | Players | Avg model | Avg public | Avg actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sources split | 12 | 16.4 | 16.2 | 18.6 |
| Sources agreed | 32 | 15.2 | 14.6 | 15.3 |

Players where the sources agreed averaged 15.3 points; the split group averaged 18.6. Over the full 192-player pool, split players averaged 13.0 vs 8.7 for the rest. The slate-wide numbers point the same way. With only 12 players in the split group this week there is no reliable conclusion either way. We will keep tracking it.

The biggest beats came from a few offenses. **Jordan Love** threw for 372 yards and 3 touchdowns, and **Matthew Golden** (10 catches, 164 yards) and **Christian Watson** (7 for 171) both cleared 33 points. **CeeDee Lamb** caught 10 of 16 targets for 195 yards, and **Puka Nacua** caught 14 of 19.

**Cam Skattebo** was tagged Doubtful and has no stat line, so he is left out of the grading (44 of 45 board players graded). Both lists above are ordinary single-week variance, which is why the grade runs over many weeks.

## Outside opinion

Players flagged bullish by logged articles and podcasts averaged 13.4 actual points across the 192 graded, versus 10.3 for neutral and 4.0 for bearish, the order you'd hope for. One week is not a trend. We will keep tracking it.

## Injury tags

6 players on the board carried an injury tag at snapshot time and averaged 18.3 points against a model average of 16.7. The model discounted them, and as a group they beat that discount. With 6 players that is an observation to track, not a rule.

## What this means

- The model did not beat the public average on this board. We will check again next week and look at where its big gaps came from.
- Across the full slate the model beat the public sources. One week is a start, not a record.
- Publishing the snapshot first and the grade afterward means the grade cannot be adjusted to fit. Bad weeks are as useful as good ones.

## FAQ

### What is this page?

The grade for the Week 9 signals post: the pre-game projections compared with actual DraftKings points, with the hits and the misses.

### Did the projections change after the games?

No. This grades the snapshot exactly as it was published before kickoff.

### Why show both the board and the full slate?

The board is the top-scoring names, a selected group, so its correlations run lower than a full slate's. The full-slate numbers cover everyone. The board covers the players the signals page featured.

### Is one week enough to judge the model?

No. A single slate is noisy, so the same snapshot and grade are published every week.
