*Results as of the Week 5 games. This grades the
[pre-game snapshot](/blog/nfl-dfs-weekly-signals/2026/week-5/) exactly as
it was published — no projection was changed after the fact.*

The pre-game post showed what the data looked like. Here is how it
graded: projections against real DraftKings points, the hits, the misses,
and a plain read on whether the model earned its place on the board.
One week is a small sample, so the point of this page is to show the
method and the numbers, not to declare a winner.

## The short version

- Across the 45-player board, the **model's projections lined up worse with real results than the
  public average did**: correlation 0.28 vs 0.54, average miss 9.1 vs 8.1 points.
  The model was closer than the public average on 17 of 45 players.
- Across **all 198 graded players** (not just the board), the picture is
  different: the model's average miss was **4.92 points** vs **5.51** for the
  four public sources (their individual scores averaged), with correlation
  **0.601** vs **0.618**.
- The board is a selected group — the top-scoring names — so its
  correlations run lower than a full slate's. That's expected, and it's
  why both views are shown.
- The biggest model misses on the board were at the high end: players
  projected in the high teens or twenties who finished in single digits.

## Board results by group

| Group | Players | Avg model | Avg public | Avg actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sources agreed | 36 | 13.6 | 14.6 | 18.2 |
| Sources split | 9 | 17.0 | 15.6 | 14.1 |

Players where the sources agreed averaged 18.2 points; the split
group averaged 14.1. Over the full 198-player pool the pattern
ran the other way (contested players averaged 10.99 vs 9.16 for the rest),
so with 9 players in the split group this week there is no
reliable conclusion either way. It is worth tracking across more weeks.

## Outside opinion

Players flagged bullish by logged articles and podcasts averaged **13.8**
actual points across the 198 graded, versus **6.9** for neutral and
**5.8** for bearish. That's the strongest result of the week, and likely
helped by having nine logged sources this week instead of the usual two or
three. One week is not a trend, but it's the signal we'll keep tracking.

## Injury tags

8 players on the board carried a Questionable tag at snapshot time
and averaged 18.8 points against a model average of
11.7. The model discounted them, and as a group they
beat that discount. With 8 players that is an observation to
track, not a rule.

## What this means

- **The model did not beat the public average on this board.** Say it
  plainly, check it again next week, and look at where the model's big
  gaps came from.
- **The model did have a lower average error across the full slate.**
  Whether that holds up is the real test.
- **Process over verdicts.** The point of publishing the snapshot before
  and the grade after is that the grading can't be bent. The weeks that
  look bad are as useful as the ones that look good.
