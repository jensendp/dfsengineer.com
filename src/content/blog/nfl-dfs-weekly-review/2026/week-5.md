The pre-game post showed what the data looked like. Here is how it
graded: projections against real DraftKings points, the hits, the misses,
and a plain read on whether the model earned its place on the board.
One week is a small sample, so the point of this page is to show the
method and the numbers, not to declare a winner.

## The short version

- On the 45-player board, the **model's projections lined up worse with real results than the public average did**: correlation 0.28 vs 0.54, average miss 9.1 vs 8.1 points. The model was closer than the public average on 17 of 45 players.
- Across **all 267 graded players** (not just the board), the model won on one measure and lost on the other: the model's average miss was **4.92 points** vs **5.51** for the 4 public sources (their individual scores averaged), with correlation **0.601** vs **0.618**.
- The board is a selected group — the top-scoring names — so its correlations run lower than a full slate's. That's expected, and it's why both views are shown.
- The largest model miss on the board was **Chris Olave** (model 24.3, actual 8.6); the largest beat was **Isaiah Likely** (model 5.4, actual 32.4).

## Board results by group

| Group | Players | Avg model | Avg public | Avg actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sources split | 9 | 17.0 | 15.6 | 14.1 |
| Sources agreed | 36 | 13.6 | 14.6 | 18.2 |

Players where the sources agreed averaged 18.2 points; the split group averaged 14.1. Over the full 198-player pool, split players averaged 11.0 vs 9.2 for the rest. The slate-wide numbers point the other way. With only 9 players in the split group this week there is no reliable conclusion either way. It is worth tracking across more weeks.

## Outside opinion

Players flagged bullish by logged articles and podcasts averaged **13.8** actual points across the 198 graded, versus **6.9** for neutral and **5.8** for bearish — the order you'd hope for. One week is not a trend, but it is a signal we will keep tracking.

## Injury tags

8 players on the board carried an injury tag at snapshot time and averaged 18.8 points against a model average of 11.7. The model discounted them, and as a group they beat that discount. With 8 players that is an observation to track, not a rule.

## What this means

- **The model did not beat the public average on this board.** Say it plainly, check it again next week, and look at where the model's big gaps came from.
- **Across the full slate the result was mixed:** the model won on one accuracy measure and lost on the other. Whether that holds up is the real test.
- **Process over verdicts.** The point of publishing the snapshot before and the grade after is that the grading can't be bent. The weeks that look bad are as useful as the ones that look good.

### Why show both the board and the full slate?

The board is the top-scoring names, a selected group, so its correlations run lower than a full slate's. The full-slate numbers show how the model did on everyone; the board shows how it did on the players the signals page actually featured.

### Is one week enough to judge the model?

No. A single slate is noisy, which is why the same snapshot and the same grade are published every week.
