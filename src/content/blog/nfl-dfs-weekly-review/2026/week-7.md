*Results as of the Week 7 games. This grades the
[pre-game snapshot](/blog/nfl-dfs-weekly-signals/2026/week-7/) as
published. No projection changed after the fact.*

The pre-game post showed what the data looked like. This one grades it:
projections against actual DraftKings points, the hits and misses, and
whether the model earned its place on the board. One week is a small
sample, so this page shows the method and the numbers and declares no
winner.

## The short version

- On the 44-player board, the model's projections lined up worse with what actually happened than the public average did: correlation 0.41 vs 0.51, average miss 6.9 vs 6.2 points. The model was closer than the public average on 16 of 44 players.
- Across all 230 graded players (not just the board), the model beat the public sources on both measures: the model's average miss was 4.59 points vs 5.00 for the 4 public sources (their individual scores averaged), with correlation 0.645 vs 0.643.
- The board is a selected group, the top-scoring names, so its correlations run lower than a full slate's. Both views are shown for that reason.
- The largest model miss on the board was Derrick Henry (model 26.1, actual 5.9). The largest beat was Bucky Irving (model 10.4, actual 27.0).

## Board results by group

| Group | Players | Avg model | Avg public | Avg actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sources split | 16 | 18.0 | 16.3 | 13.9 |
| Sources agreed | 28 | 16.1 | 15.3 | 15.1 |

Players where the sources agreed averaged 15.1 points; the split group averaged 13.9. Over the full 178-player pool, split players averaged 11.4 vs 9.4 for the rest. The slate-wide numbers point the other way. With only 16 players in the split group this week there is no reliable conclusion either way. We will keep tracking it.

## Outside opinion

Players flagged bullish by logged articles and podcasts averaged 13.3 actual points across the 178 graded, versus 8.9 for neutral and 5.7 for bearish, the order you'd hope for. One week is not a trend. We will keep tracking it.

## Injury tags

4 players on the board carried an injury tag at snapshot time and averaged 17.4 points against a model average of 16.1. The model discounted them, and as a group they beat that discount. With 4 players that is an observation to track, not a rule.

## What this means

- The model did not beat the public average on this board. We will check again next week and look at where its big gaps came from.
- Across the full slate the model beat the public sources. One week is a start, not a record.
- Publishing the snapshot first and the grade afterward means the grade cannot be adjusted to fit. Bad weeks are as useful as good ones.
