*Results as of the Week 11 games. This grades the
[pre-game snapshot](/blog/nfl-dfs-weekly-signals/2026/week-11/) as
published. No projection changed after the fact.*

The pre-game post showed what the data looked like. This one grades it:
projections against actual DraftKings points, the hits and misses, and
whether the model earned its place on the board. One week is a small
sample, so this page shows the method and the numbers and declares no
winner.

## The short version

- On the 45-player board, the model's projections lined up worse with what actually happened than the public average did: correlation 0.59 vs 0.70, average miss 6.5 vs 6.5 points. The model was closer than the public average on 26 of 45 players.
- Across all 234 graded players (not just the board), the model won on one measure and lost on the other: the model's average miss was 4.38 points vs 4.60 for the 4 public sources (their individual scores averaged), with correlation 0.679 vs 0.703.
- The board is a selected group, the top-scoring names, so its correlations run lower than a full slate's. Both views are shown for that reason.
- The largest model miss on the board was Kyle Monangai (model 14.4, actual 1.8). The largest beat was Jonah Coleman (model 14.4, actual 37.9).

## Board results by group

| Group | Players | Avg model | Avg public | Avg actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sources split | 13 | 18.3 | 17.9 | 17.6 |
| Sources agreed | 32 | 14.1 | 14.4 | 14.4 |

Players where the sources agreed averaged 14.4 points; the split group averaged 17.6. Over the full 180-player pool, split players averaged 12.7 vs 8.5 for the rest. The slate-wide numbers point the same way. With only 13 players in the split group this week there is no reliable conclusion either way. We will keep tracking it.

## Outside opinion

Players flagged bullish by logged articles and podcasts averaged 12.4 actual points across the 180 graded, versus 8.8 for neutral and 4.9 for bearish, the order you'd hope for. One week is not a trend. We will keep tracking it.

## Injury tags

1 player on the board carried an injury tag at snapshot time and averaged 13.8 points against a model average of 11.2. The model discounted them, and as a group they beat that discount. With 1 player that is an observation to track, not a rule.

## What this means

- The model did not beat the public average on this board. We will check again next week and look at where its big gaps came from.
- Across the full slate the result was mixed: the model won on one accuracy measure and lost on the other. Whether that holds over more weeks is the test.
- Publishing the snapshot first and the grade afterward means the grade cannot be adjusted to fit. Bad weeks are as useful as good ones.
