## What changed on August 30

The season hasn't started, so every projection on this site is
necessarily built on prior-season data — that's not new. What changed
is a real gap in *how* that prior-season data was being used: this
model was treating a player's role from last season as if it still
applied today, even when it demonstrably doesn't anymore. Two concrete,
verified examples that triggered this fix: James Conner's projection
was built on 10 real 2025 games as Arizona's back — all of them before
Arizona's own preseason depth chart moved a rookie ahead of him this
year. Kyler Murray's projection was 100% Arizona production, even
though he's now on Minnesota's roster with zero real signal on how he
fits that offense.

Two real, graduated discounts now apply on top of the underlying
recent-form data, not instead of it: one for every real depth-chart
step below #1 at a position (a demonstrated drop, not last year's
number carried forward blind), and a separate one for any player whose
entire projection window predates an offseason team change. Both are
grounded in actual data, not guesses — full reasoning is in the FAQ
below. The result: David Njoku, James Conner, and several other
previously-listed names dropped off this board entirely — legitimately
discounted for a real, current-season reason, not deleted. 21 of the 22
real plays below are now a confirmed #1 at their position — Michael
Wilson is the one exception, and he earns it on real target-share data
even after the discount, not by default.

## FAQ

### Why isn't every high-value name on this board a pick?

Because "value" only measures projected points against salary — it
doesn't know whether a player's current role actually supports getting
those points. This week's board is checked against real, team-reported
depth-chart data before any role gets described as a "starter" or
similar; when that data doesn't back the number up, this post says so
directly instead of quietly including the name anyway.

### Does an injury designation actually change the numbers?

Yes, directly. A real "Questionable," "Doubtful," or "Out" status on a
player pulls their projection down by a set amount before they show up
on this board at all. It's not hidden and it's not a footnote; it's
already priced into the number you're looking at.

### Are deep-bench backups filtered out of this board?

Yes, in two layers now. Any player whose only real recent history is
three-plus seasons old, or whose current team-reported depth-chart spot
is clearly buried at their position (QB3+, RB4+, WR5+, TE4+), gets
capped to a replacement-level baseline and excluded from the ranked
board outright — not just capped and left rankable on cheap salary.

### Why did some previously-listed value plays disappear this update?

Because of the August 30 model change (see above), not because they got
worse. Any player below #1 at their position now carries a real,
graduated discount, and any player whose entire projection history
predates an offseason team change carries a separate discount on top of
that. Both are grounded in real data — a same-week cross-check against
DK's own published FPPG for confirmed team-changed players — not a
guess.

### Does the matchup affect this week's rankings?

Mostly not, with one real exception. Most of this week's top values carry
a neutral matchup multiplier (1.0x) — the value is coming from the
salary-to-projection gap itself. Jacksonville's defense is the one clear
exception this week (1.61x), where the matchup itself is doing real work,
not just the price.

### When does this get updated before kickoff?

Whenever a salary, projection, or depth-chart change is significant
enough to matter. Check the "as of" line at the top of the post — it
always reflects the most recent real update, not just when this was
first published.
