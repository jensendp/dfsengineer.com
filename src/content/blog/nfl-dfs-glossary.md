You'll see most of these terms used without explanation once you're in a
few DFS threads or articles — this is the one place to look them up
instead of guessing from context.

## Ownership

The percentage of lineups in a contest expected to roster a given player.
25% ownership means roughly 1 in 4 entries has that player. Matters most
in GPPs, where your score only counts relative to the field.

## Chalk

A player, team, or game projected to be highly owned. The "obvious" plays
most of the field is on. Not a bad thing by itself — chalk is often chalk
because it's a genuinely good play — but stacking too much of it in a GPP
means you're not differentiating from anyone.

## Leverage

Rostering a lower-owned player close in projection to a more popular one,
so when your guy hits, you gain ground on the whole field stuck on the
chalk.

## Ceiling

A player's realistic best-case score for the week. What matters most in
GPPs, where you need a spike, not just a solid outcome.

## Floor

A player's realistic worst-case score for the week — assuming things go
about as poorly as they reasonably could, not a total bust. What matters
most in cash games, where consistency beats upside.

## Game Script

The flow of a game based on the score, and how it changes usage. A team
protecting a big lead leans run-heavy late; a team playing from behind
throws more.

## Slate

The specific set of games included in a contest. A "main slate" covers
most of Sunday's early and late games; a single-game (showdown) slate is
built around just one matchup, with a much smaller player pool.

## GPP

Short for Guaranteed Prize Pool — a tournament with a fixed total payout
regardless of entries, paid out top-heavy to a small share of the field.
The opposite structure from a cash game, where roughly half the field
cashes at a flat rate.

## Stack

Rostering a QB with one of his own pass-catchers, so their scoring is
linked — when he throws a touchdown, someone in your lineup catches it.
Adding a pass-catcher from the *opposing* team on top of that is a
bring-back, betting the whole game turns into a shootout.

## Punt

Deliberately rostering a low-salary, low-projection player at one spot to
free up cap space for stronger plays elsewhere. Not a mistake — a
deliberate tradeoff.
